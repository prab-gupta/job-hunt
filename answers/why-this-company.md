# "Why this company / why do you want to work here?"

The formula that worked: **one accurate sentence about their product** (from their site, never
guessed) + **the honest overlap with what he's actually done**. Never recycle a paragraph
verbatim to the same employer; adapt per role.

## Sent to Oh (MarTech Engineer) — 2026-08-05

> Because the job description is the work I've been doing, but with a bigger surface to do it
> on. Wiring Make and Zapier flows, standing up LangChain or CrewAI agents, and keeping the
> data clean between marketing tools and the CRM is exactly what my last two years looked
> like — the difference is that at FlowSpace I was doing it alongside product and migration
> work, and here it's the whole role. I'm most useful in the gap between "marketing has an
> idea" and "the system actually does it reliably", and that gap is what this role owns.

## Sent to OpenHands (Enterprise Agent Engineer) — 2026-08-05

> I've spent the last two years building agents that have to work in front of real users
> rather than in a demo, and this role is that job at a company whose product is the agent
> itself. The enterprise angle is what draws me: the hard part of the agents I've shipped was
> never the model, it was the integration surface — the CRM that returns a different shape
> than the docs promise, the booking API that rate-limits at the wrong moment, the support
> flow that has to fail gracefully when the agent doesn't know. That's the work I want more of.

## Sent to Viktor (Agent Harness Engineer) — 2026-08-05

> I want to work on the layer between a model and something people can rely on, at a company
> where that layer is the product rather than an internal tool.

## Sent to Duvo (AI Platform Engineer) — 2026-08-05

> What makes me a fit is that I've built the unglamorous half of AI products: the
> integrations, the data pipelines and the agent plumbing that decide whether a feature works
