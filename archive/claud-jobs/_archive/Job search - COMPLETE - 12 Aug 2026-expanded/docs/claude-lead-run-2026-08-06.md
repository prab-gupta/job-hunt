## Where the honest answer cost him a role, three times

**capital.com (Cyprus)** and **Wiz (Spain)** both use a closed dropdown of work-permit types. Neither list
contains anything that describes an Italian EU long-term residence permit with Article 14 mobility, so
every option would have been a false statement. Both forms were filled and abandoned.

**ActiveCampaign (Krakow)** wants his permit type and its exact expiry date, which is in no file I hold.

Set against that, several forms asked a plain yes or no about EU work authorisation, and there his
standing rule applies: yes for any EU or EEA country, no sponsorship. That is what went in for Poland,
Czechia, Bulgaria, Greece, Germany and Spain-adjacent reqs, and each one is noted on its row.

**One role was held on judgement rather than eligibility.** Lodgify's Senior Growth Engineer screens on
four things he would truthfully answer No to, including the GA4 and consent-governance gap named in the
Sporty Group letter. It was held in favour of their Senior AI Engineer req at the same company, which
fits him properly, so that application is not diluted. Say the word and both go out.
